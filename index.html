Equity intelligence on talent. See where someone's hire-grant stock likely stands today, how much is still vesting and when, and what that suggests about whether to reach out and how to shape the conversation.

Estimates draw on public share prices and standard vesting terms. They exclude refresh grants, discretionary awards and sign-on compensation, which companies do not disclose publicly.

How to use it

  1. Type the company the person works at now.
  2. Pick the month and year they started there. Their LinkedIn profile usually shows it.
  3. Click Look up.

What you'll learn

  • Whether the stock they received at hire is now worth less than its value at grant (underwater).
  • How much of it is still unvested, and when it finishes vesting.
  • How strongly that equity is likely holding them, and what that means for your pitch.
  • Bonus and stock-refresh dates. Departures tend to follow shortly after these payouts.

Works for people at publicly traded companies. Startups and private companies don't have public stock prices, so they can't be looked up.

Requested companies are added at the Monday refresh, if they trade on a public stock exchange.

How the estimate works

  • Grant price: the average of the month-end stock closes for the month before they started and their start month. Companies usually price new-hire grants on an average like this.
  • Underwater means today's price is below that grant price. Within 15% above it counts as near the line.
  • Vesting uses each company's own new-hire schedule, including uneven ones like Amazon's 5/15/40/40, from SEC filings or Levels.fyi. Each result says whether the schedule is sourced, partly sourced, or a guess. With no source on file, it assumes 4 years with a 1-year cliff and labels that as a guess.
  • Holding power is the unvested part of the hire grant valued at today's price, shown per $100,000 granted. It leaves out refreshers, promotions and bonuses, which this tool cannot see.
  • Month-end prices from Digrin, adjusted for splits, refreshed every Monday.

These are estimates from public stock data and typical vesting, not a person's actual grant. Use them to inform your approach, not to state as fact to the person. Not financial advice.
